Abstract

We study drawer and charger insertion with 0.7–2 mm clearances on a roughly $1,000 arm with high joint friction and joint play. Simulators constructed from a few real demonstrations supply state-based teachers for image-based students. Students trained with scripted teachers complete 12/20 drawer trials and 18/20 charger trials on the real arm. The RL-teacher drawer student completes 6/20 trials. Three lessons emerge: directly revising scripted teachers shortens development, smoothing teacher decisions makes supervision more consistent, and commanded and measured joint positions provide a cue for contact-guided insertion.

Task and Setup

Precise insertion must tolerate an imperfect arm and an imperfect simulator. Even after calibration, the best rigid model misplaces the wrist camera by a median 10 mm. Within simulation, changing the timestep or switching between CPU and GPU shifts the final drawer position by 1–2 cm under the same action sequence. Both effects are larger than the insertion clearances.

Training Pipeline

We fit the drawer simulator to ten real demonstrations and build the charger model from one, measuring geometry in its images. A coding agent writes and revises a Python controller that reads simulator state to choose grasps, motions, and recovery actions.

The student practices in simulation using online DAgger, in which the teacher supplies actions for the states the student visits. The student uses scene and wrist images, commanded joints, and measured joints. It predicts short sequences of actions and runs on the real arm using these same inputs.

Experimental Analysis

Direct revision of scripted teachers

Our reinforcement learning teachers found edge grasps that depended on support from the cabinet. In simulated free-hanging tests, edge grasps dropped the drawer in 11–12 of 12 trials, compared with 1 of 12 for centre grasps. Scripted teachers let us change grasps and recovery rules directly, without retraining the teacher.

Coding agents assisted both approaches. In this project, developing scripts took roughly two days, compared with two weeks spent developing RL teachers.

Teacher learnability

A teacher can solve the task using exact simulator state yet give opposite commands for views the student cannot tell apart. An early student repeatedly opened and closed its gripper on the real drawer. We smoothed the teacher’s decisions and aligned its action sequences with the student’s execution. Gripper disagreement under pose perturbations fell from 28.6% to 0.4%.

We perturb the drawer and cabinet poses across 16 copies of a state by 5 mm and 1°. The arm executes the median joint action and the majority gripper vote. The test asks whether the teacher still succeeds when nearby states produce slightly different decisions, without first training a camera student.

Perturbed-teacher successes are 0/1024, 126/1024, and 784/1024. Student successes are 118/349, 471/766, and 814/979. These are simulation measurements across successive revisions, separate from the real trials in Table 1.

Contact-guided recovery

When the arm meets resistance, its measured joints can fall behind their commanded positions. Under joint PD control, the difference between commanded joints, qdes, and measured joints, q, gives the policy a cue about contact, without torque sensors.

The drawer teacher uses commanded and measured motion to retreat, realign, and push again. The deployed student receives those same signals. Light contact remains difficult to distinguish from joint friction.

How Compliance Helps Insertion

Finite-gain joint PD control lets the arm yield when contact blocks its commanded motion. Maintaining pressure while changing alignment lets the surface guide the object toward the opening.
