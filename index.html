The challenge

The last millimeter
is smaller than the error.

Precise insertion must tolerate an imperfect arm and an imperfect simulator.

Our arm has high joint friction and joint play. Even after calibration, the best rigid model misplaces the wrist camera by a median 10 mm. Within simulation, changing the timestep or switching between CPU and GPU shifts the final drawer position by 1–2 cm under the same action sequence.

Both effects are larger than the insertion clearances. The policy must use feedback to finish the task.

The method

Real demonstrations.
Simulated practice. Real execution.

01 / OBSERVE

Build the simulator

Fit the drawer model to ten real demonstrations. Build the charger model from one, measuring geometry in its images.

02 / TEACH

Write a teacher

A coding agent writes and revises a Python controller. It reads simulator state to choose grasps, motions, and recovery actions.

03 / LEARN

Train a camera policy

The student practices in simulation. The teacher supplies actions for the states it visits, using online DAgger.

04 / TRANSFER

Run on the real arm

The student uses scene and wrist images, commanded joints, and measured joints. It predicts short sequences of actions.

What we learned

Three lessons for precise transfer.

01

Revise the teacher directly.

Our reinforcement learning teachers found edge grasps that depended on support from the cabinet. Scripted teachers let us change grasps and recovery rules directly, without retraining the teacher.

Coding agents assisted both approaches. In this project, developing scripts took roughly two days, compared with two weeks spent developing RL teachers.

In simulated free-hanging tests, edge grasps dropped the drawer in 11–12 of 12 trials, compared with 1 of 12 for centre grasps.

02

A good teacher must be learnable.

A teacher can solve the task using exact simulator state yet give opposite commands for views the student cannot tell apart. An early student repeatedly opened and closed its gripper on the real drawer.

We smoothed the teacher’s decisions and aligned its action sequences with the student’s execution. Gripper disagreement under pose perturbations fell from 28.6% to 0.4%.

03

Use contact to correct alignment.

When the arm meets resistance, its measured joints can fall behind their commanded positions. Under joint PD control, this gap gives the policy a cue about contact.

The drawer teacher uses commanded and measured motion to retreat, realign, and push again. The deployed student receives those same signals. Light contact remains difficult to distinguish from joint friction.

The precise step and the final push are different.

Recorded drawer failures include entering the opening successfully, then holding the drawer for a long time without releasing it for the final push. Completing the whole task remains the criterion.

These results support learnable, contact-aware scripted teachers as a practical starting point for precise insertion on a low-cost arm.

How we test whether a teacher is learnable

We perturb the drawer and cabinet poses across 16 copies of a state by 5 mm and 1°. The arm executes the median joint action and the majority gripper vote. The test asks whether the teacher still succeeds when nearby states produce slightly different decisions, without first training a camera student.
